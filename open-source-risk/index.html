Blind spot

Direct versus transitive dependencies

Chosen by a person, reviewed at some point

Direct dependencies

Installed automatically, reviewed by no one

Transitive dependencies

Illustrative proportions. In most package ecosystems the majority of shipped components are pulled in indirectly.

Three consequences

No approval gate — the decision was made by a manifest file, not a reviewer.

No owner — when it breaks, there is no team name attached to it.

No signal — a maintainer change or a new owner reaches us silently.

Sustainability

The maintainer problem

90%

of codebases carry components that are out of date, unmaintained, or both.

Critical libraries are often maintained by one unpaid person, in their own time.

Burnout is an attack surface — the xz operation targeted exactly that.

When a component stops moving, there is no vendor to escalate to. The options are fund it, fork it, or replace it.

Source: Black Duck OSSRA 2026.

Part four

What good looks like

Controls

Five controls that matter

01

An SBOM for every build

Generated in the pipeline, stored, and queryable in minutes — not assembled by hand during an incident.

02

An owner for every critical component

A named team for the components that carry the business, transitive ones included.

03

Upgrade SLAs by severity

Agreed with engineering, funded as ordinary work, and measured — patching is a capacity decision, not a virtue.

04

A hardened build pipeline

Pinned versions, verified provenance, scoped tokens, and no unreviewed install scripts.

05

A plan for unmaintained code

For each end-of-life dependency: pay for support, fund the maintainer, fork it, or replace it.

Sequence

The first ninety days

Days 1–30

See the estate

Generate SBOMs for the twenty systems that carry revenue. Name one accountable owner for the programme.

Days 31–60

Rank the exposure

Sort by internet-facing, critical severity, and unmaintained. Agree upgrade SLAs with engineering leadership.

Days 61–90

Close the loop

Wire the CRA reporting path end to end. Report coverage and mean time to upgrade to the board each quarter.

Two metrics tell us whether this is working: what share of builds produce an SBOM, and how long a critical fix takes to reach production.
